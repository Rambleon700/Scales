Anatomy of Major & Minor Scale Families

All Western musical scales are constructed using specific sequences of Whole-steps (W = 2 frets) and Half-steps (H = 1 fret) from a root note.

Scale / Mode Step Formula Interval Structure Key Quality
Major (Ionian) W - W - H - W - W - W - H 1 - 2 - 3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - 7 Bright, Triumphant, Standard Major
Natural Minor (Aeolian) W - H - W - W - H - W - W 1 - 2 - b3 - 4 - 5 - b6 - b7 Sad, Melancholic, Standard Minor
Harmonic Minor W - H - W - W - H - 1.5W - H 1 - 2 - b3 - 4 - 5 - b6 - 7 Exotic, Neoclassical, Spanish
Melodic Minor (Jazz) W - H - W - W - W - W - H 1 - 2 - b3 - 4 - 5 - 6 - 7 Sophisticated, Modern Jazz
Minor Pentatonic 1.5W - W - W - 1.5W - W 1 - b3 - 4 - 5 - b7 Blues, Rock, Universal Lead

The Bright-to-Dark Spectrum of Modes

The 7 diatonic modes derived from the Major scale can be arranged in order from Brightest (most raised intervals) to Darkest (most lowered/flatted intervals):

1. Lydian (Brightest)

Formula: 1 2 3 #4 5 6 7. Dreamy, cinematic, raised 4th.

2. Ionian (Major)

Formula: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7. Pure, stable, resolved.

3. Mixolydian

Formula: 1 2 3 4 5 6 b7. Bluesy major, southern rock.

4. Dorian

Formula: 1 2 b3 4 5 6 b7. Jazzy, funky minor with major 6th.

5. Aeolian (Minor)

Formula: 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7. Emotional, heavy, natural minor.

6. Phrygian

Formula: 1 b2 b3 4 5 b6 b7. Dark, Spanish, metal b2 interval.

CAGED vs. 3-Notes-Per-String (3NPS) Systems

Two primary frameworks exist for navigating scales across the guitar fretboard:

The CAGED System

Maps scale shapes directly over open chord shapes (C, A, G, E, D). Box shapes stay within a 4-5 fret span, making it ideal for blues, chord-melody, and localized bending.

3-Notes-Per-String (3NPS)

Strict consistency: exactly 3 notes are played on every string. This creates symmetrical picking patterns, making it superior for high-speed legato, alternate picking, and sweeping across the neck.

Speed & Technique Practice Routines

Drill 1: Groups of 4 Sequence

Play notes 1-2-3-4, then starting from note 2 play 2-3-4-5, then 3-4-5-6 up the scale. Builds alternate picking speed and finger independence.

Drill 2: String Skipping Arpeggiations

Play the scale notes on String 6, skip String 5 to play String 4, then String 5 to String 3. Develops precise pick targeting.
